In 1970 Walter Mischel put a marshmallow in front of a four-year-old and left the room. Follow-up studies reported that the children who waited had higher test scores and better outcomes as teenagers. The finding became parenting advice, curriculum, corporate training, and a shorthand for character. The instruction that came out of it was to practise waiting, because waiting is what successful people do.

That instruction treats delay of gratification as an input, as a measurement.

## What waiting measures

Celeste Kidd, Holly Palmeri and Richard Aslin ran the marshmallow task with one change. Before the test, an adult made a promise to each child and then either kept it or broke it. Children who had seen the promise broken waited a mean of 3 minutes 2 seconds. Children who had seen it kept waited 12 minutes 2 seconds. One of fourteen children in the unreliable condition held out the full fifteen minutes; nine of fourteen did in the reliable condition. The sample was 28 children, mean age four and a half.

A single broken promise moved the measurement by nine minutes. The children in the unreliable condition were reading their situation accurately. A second marshmallow that does not arrive is worth less than a first marshmallow on the table.

In 2018 Tyler Watts, Greg Duncan and Haonan Quan ran a conceptual replication on 918 children from the NICHD longitudinal study. The association between waiting at age four and achievement at fifteen was about half the size reported originally. Adding controls for family background, early cognitive ability and the home environment cut it by roughly two thirds of what remained. For children whose mothers had not completed college, an extra minute of waiting predicted about a tenth of a standard deviation in achievement.

The wait time carries information about the household the child came from, and those household factors were predictive about future success.

## The trait that is not a struggle

Wilhelm Hofmann, Kathleen Vohs and Roy Baumeister gave 205 adults handheld devices and sampled their desires across a week, collecting 7,827 reports. People scoring high on trait self-control reported weaker desires, less internal conflict, and less resistance. They were not winning more fights. They were in fewer of them.

Brian Galla and Angela Duckworth followed this across six studies with 2,274 participants. Self-control predicted outcomes such as grade point average and first-year college persistence, and habits mediated the relationship. Higher self-control went with less effortful inhibition and more automaticity in eating, exercise, sleep and study.

Grit, the adjacent construct, was meta-analysed by Marcus Credé, Michael Tynan and Peter Harms across 584 effect sizes, 88 samples and 66,807 individuals. Grit correlated moderately with performance and retention, and very strongly with conscientiousness. The authors concluded that interventions designed to raise grit are likely to have weak effects on performance.

The measured trait tracks conditions and established routine. Training the measurement does not reproduce the thing being measured.

## ADHD

Edmund Sonuga-Barke's dual pathway model describes ADHD as involving two partly separable things: executive dysfunction, and delay aversion. Delay aversion is a motivational state. Waiting produces a negative affective state, and behaviour is organised around escaping it. The impulsive choice is the exit from something that feels bad.

Jackson and MacKillop meta-analysed 21 studies, 25 case-control comparisons, combined N of 3,913. People with ADHD discount delayed monetary rewards more steeply, Cohen's d of 0.43, with minimal heterogeneity and little sign of publication bias.

The associated life outcomes are documented. Danckaerts and colleagues reviewed quality of life in children with ADHD and found substantial reductions across physical, psychosocial and school domains. Lee and colleagues meta-analysed 27 longitudinal studies: childhood ADHD is associated with roughly twice the risk of nicotine and alcohol use disorders, around three times the risk of nicotine dependence, and more than two and a half times the risk of any substance use disorder.

The direction of the relationship is testable, because ADHD medication is started, stopped and restarted within the same person over time.

Paul Lichtenstein and colleagues used Swedish national registers covering more than 25,000 people with ADHD between 2006 and 2009, comparing each person's medicated periods against their own unmedicated periods. Criminality was 32% lower during medicated periods for men (hazard ratio 0.68, 95% CI 0.63–0.73) and 41% lower for women (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.50–0.70). The effect held for petty and violent offences.

Zheng Chang and colleagues ran the same design on 38,753 people for substance abuse outcomes: 31% lower during medicated periods, hazard ratio 0.69, 95% CI 0.57–0.84. Timothy Wilens and colleagues meta-analysed stimulant treatment and later substance use disorder and found treated youth at reduced risk relative to untreated youth, roughly 1.9-fold lower in studies following into adulthood.

## Other states

Michael Amlung and colleagues meta-analysed delay discounting across psychiatric diagnoses in JAMA Psychiatry: 43 studies, 57 effect sizes. Major depressive disorder showed steeper discounting than controls, Hedges g of 0.37 (95% CI 0.14–0.61, N of 420), with elevations also in bipolar disorder, borderline personality disorder and binge eating disorder. A published commentary in the same journal disputes how far the transdiagnostic claim generalises, so the specific effect sizes are firmer than the overall framing.

Anandi Mani, Sendhil Mullainathan, Eldar Shafir and Jiaying Zhao tested Indian sugarcane farmers before and after harvest. The same farmers scored lower on cognitive tasks in the weeks when they were poor than in the weeks when they were paid.

Sleep needs a distinction. Ryan Meldrum, J.C. Barnes and Carter Hay followed roughly 825 young people to age fifteen and found sleep deprivation predicting lower self-control, which predicted delinquency. Michael Christian and Aleksander Ellis found shift-level sleep below six hours predicting workplace deviance in 171 nurses. A 2022 review by Downey and colleagues found sleep deprivation has consistently null effects on monetary delay discounting specifically, with Hedges g between −0.21 and 0.07. Sleep loss shows up in behavioural self-regulation and does not show up in the economic discounting measure.

Moving the conditions moves the behaviour. Jane Costello and colleagues tracked 1,420 rural children through a period when a casino opened on a reservation and began distributing unconditional income to tribal members. Among children whose families rose out of poverty, symptoms of conduct and oppositional disorders fell by about 40%, converging on the rates of children who had never been poor. Anxiety and depression symptoms did not move. The change was specific to the externalising, self-control-related symptoms.

Johannes Haushofer and Jeremy Shapiro's randomised trial of unconditional cash transfers in rural Kenya found large increases in psychological wellbeing and reductions in cortisol in some subgroups. A meta-analysis by Joel McGuire, Caspar Kaiser and Anders Bach-Mortensen across 45 studies and 116,999 people found cash transfers producing small positive effects on subjective wellbeing (d of 0.13) and mental health (d of 0.07), scaling with transfer size relative to recipient income.

## The same inference in engineering

On 1 August 2012 Knight Capital deployed new order-routing code to eight servers. A technician copied it to seven. No second technician reviewed the deployment. The eighth server still carried a retired function called Power Peg, and the new code repurposed a flag that switched it on. Over 45 minutes the system executed 4 million orders across 154 stocks and more than 397 million shares, leaving the firm with a $3.5 billion net long position and a $3.15 billion net short position. The loss exceeded $460 million. Starting at 8:01am, 97 automated emails reported "Power Peg disabled". They were not built as alerts and nobody read them before the open. Engineers responding to the incident uninstalled the new code from the seven working servers, which activated the same dormant function there. The SEC fined the firm $12 million under the Market Access Rule.

The record in the SEC order is a manual multi-server deployment with no second reviewer, dead code left reachable in production for nine years, a safety check relocated in 2005 without retesting, and a warning channel that produced 97 messages nobody was assigned to read.

The Columbia Accident Investigation Board wrote that NASA was "accepting more and more risk in order to stay on schedule", and that foam shedding had come to be regarded as inevitable or as an acceptable risk. The board adopted Diane Vaughan's term for it, normalisation of deviance, from her analysis of Challenger seventeen years earlier. The Rogers Commission had recorded a propensity to defer known safety problems. The 2020 House Transportation Committee report on the 737 MAX describes financial pressure to compete on schedule, compressed testing, and a business commitment to avoid simulator training requirements. The 2019 Joint Authorities Technical Review found indications that engineers certifying the aircraft on the FAA's behalf faced pressure from managers prioritising cost and schedule, and recommended communication channels free of fear of punitive action.

Technical debt behaves the same way. Terese Besker, Antonio Martini and Jan Bosch tracked 43 developers reporting twice weekly over seven weeks and found an average 23% of development time lost to existing technical debt, with the debt forcing the introduction of further debt. Their 2019 replication across a broader set of practitioner roles put the figure at 36%. Developers were aware of the loss. Managers were not.

A 2024 study of 35 security incident responders at a large IT company found 19 of them meeting criteria for burnout, associated with workload, limited control, time pressure and lack of management support. The burned-out responders worked more than 40 hours a week, slept worse, and did more after-hours work.

## What the working arrangements do

Google's SRE practice caps aggregate operations work for SREs at 50%. When a team's operational load runs over the cap for a sustained period, the excess is routed back to the product development team: tickets are reassigned, developers rejoin the on-call rotation. The error budget follows the same shape, defined as one minus the availability target, which makes a quantity of failure an expected part of shipping rather than a matter of individual carefulness.

Toyota's andon cord is often described as any worker stopping the factory. What the cord does is sound an alarm and turn on a yellow light. A team leader has between five and thirty seconds to respond before the line reaches its fixed stopping position, and if the issue is resolved and the cord re-pulled inside that window, the line does not stop. Buffers between segments keep the rest of the plant running when a segment does halt. The point is that calling for help is cheap and carries no penalty.

Google's Project Aristotle studied around 180 teams using over 35 statistical models. The published conclusion is that who is on a team matters less than how team members interact, with psychological safety the strongest of five predictors. Amy Edmondson's 1999 study of 51 work teams found psychological safety associated with learning behaviour, and learning behaviour mediating the relationship to team performance. DORA's State of DevOps research reports a culture of psychological safety as predictive of software delivery performance, organisational performance and productivity. DORA's own language is "predictive of" throughout; the work is survey-based and correlational, and it does not establish direction on its own.

## The question

The pattern is shared across the clinical work, the economic work and the engineering record. Restraint, patience, care and long-horizon investment are what people produce when their conditions are stable enough to make those behaviours worth producing. Chasing the behaviour directly is chasing a reading on a dial - chasing the outcome before understanding the inputs that create that outcome.

The harder question, and one this post does not answer, is what actually contributes to sustained wellbeing — the kind that holds over years or decades rather than weeks — for a person, and for a team, and for a society?
